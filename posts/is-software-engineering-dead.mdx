“Software engineering is dead.”

It sounds dramatic. But if your career plan depends on getting paid well just for writing codes, take the warning seriously.

AI is changing what companies expect from devs. Knowing a programming language is still useful. It is becoming weaker advantage on its own.

## The pressure is real

Getting started in tech has become harder. [SignalFire's 2026 talent report](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2026) puts entry-level hiring roughly 65% below 2019 levels at major tech companies and 76% lower at early-stage startups.

That measures hiring, not jobs disappearing. The same report shows engineering holding up better than many other roles. It does not prove that AI caused every layoff.

Still, thee pressure to do more with smaller teams is clear. If AI can handle routine coding, devs need to bring more to the table.

## Reviewing AI code is only part of the job

You often hear the same reply: "We will always need humans to check the code."

Today, checking AI output matters. But I would not build my whole career around the idea that AI will always need the same level of help.

AI is already handling difficult reasoning tasks. In 2025, an advanced Gemini model [reached gold-medal standard at the International Mathematical Olympiad](https://deepmind.google/blog/advanced-version-of-gemini-with-deep-think-officially-achieves-gold-medal-standard-at-the-international-mathematical-olympiad/). That does not mean it can run a software project alone, but it is a reason to take its progress seriously.

## Think like a builder

From a business owners point of view, software is a way to get a result. Customers want easier payments, faster service, or less manual work. They rarely care how many lines of code it takes.

My view is that high pay for routine coding alone will become harder to justify. The stronger position is to understand a problem, use AI to build a solution, and make sure it works for real people.

For example, help a small business automate appointment reminders. Talk to the owner, find out where bookings get missed, build the tool with AI, and check whether it saves time.

That teaches you more than collecting another language on your resume.

## What is your next move?

Keep learning the basics. Learn how systems work, how to spot mistakes, and how to explain what you need. Use AI to plan, build, test, and improve a small product.

You do not have to become a founder tomorrow. Start by owning a problem from the first conversation to a working solution.

**Will you keep measuring your skills by the code you write, or by the problems you solve with AI?**
